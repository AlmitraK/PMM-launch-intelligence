# SigNoz — Company Context

## What SigNoz is

SigNoz is an open-source, OpenTelemetry-native observability platform for engineering teams. It brings application and infrastructure telemetry — logs, metrics, traces, exceptions, alerts, and related observability workflows — into one platform.

SigNoz can be consumed as a managed cloud service or self-hosted. Its open-source and OpenTelemetry-native approach is central to its positioning: customers can standardize instrumentation on open standards rather than proprietary vendor agents.

## Core customer problem

Modern engineering teams often operate fragmented observability stacks across APM, logs, metrics, infrastructure monitoring, and specialized tools. This creates:

- fragmented visibility across signals
- context switching during troubleshooting
- operational overhead from maintaining multiple tools
- unpredictable observability costs
- vendor lock-in through proprietary instrumentation
- difficulty scaling observability across teams and workloads

SigNoz aims to provide a unified observability platform that reduces this fragmentation while keeping instrumentation vendor-neutral.

## Strategic themes

### 1. Unified observability
Bring logs, metrics, traces, APM, infrastructure monitoring, alerts, exceptions, and emerging workloads such as LLM observability into one platform.

### 2. OpenTelemetry and open standards
SigNoz is built natively around OpenTelemetry. Vendor-neutral instrumentation is positioned as a long-term company asset rather than something tied to a proprietary observability vendor.

### 3. Predictable economics
SigNoz uses usage-based pricing rather than host- or seat-based pricing. Cost predictability and lower TCO are important in competitive displacement motions, particularly against established observability vendors.

### 4. Cloud and self-hosted deployment
Customers can use SigNoz Cloud or deploy SigNoz themselves. Self-hosting matters particularly for customers with security, compliance, data residency, or infrastructure-control requirements.

### 5. Enterprise readiness
As customers expand, requirements can include scale, RBAC, SSO/SAML, compliance, SLAs, migration support, data residency, and dedicated support.

### 6. Observability for AI-era workloads
SigNoz is expanding observability beyond traditional applications and infrastructure to LLM and AI-agent workloads, while correlating AI telemetry with the rest of the application stack.

## Typical competitive context

SigNoz frequently appears in evaluation or displacement contexts involving:

- Datadog
- New Relic
- Grafana-based stacks
- Prometheus / Loki / Tempo combinations
- Splunk
- other specialized observability tools

The competitive argument should not be reduced to price alone. Important dimensions include unified telemetry, OpenTelemetry-native architecture, open source, correlation across signals, deployment flexibility, scale, enterprise readiness, and predictable economics.

## Buying motion

SigNoz can enter bottom-up through engineers or technical teams and expand into a broader enterprise evaluation.

A typical enterprise journey may involve:

1. Discovery or incumbent displacement
2. Technical evaluation / proof of concept
3. Success criteria and production validation
4. Security and legal review
5. Commercial negotiation and procurement
6. Expansion and standardization

The technical champion is often a Platform Engineer, SRE Lead, Founding Engineer, or similar technical operator. Economic approval commonly moves toward CTO / VP Engineering / Platform leadership, with Security, Compliance, Procurement, and Finance becoming involved as the deployment grows.

## Guidance for the Launch Planner

When creating launch strategy:

- Treat SigNoz as an engineering infrastructure product, not generic B2B SaaS.
- Prefer concrete technical workflows and engineering outcomes over vague marketing language.
- Distinguish between the technical user, champion, economic buyer, and enterprise gatekeepers.
- Consider whether a launch primarily drives adoption, competitive displacement, expansion, or enterprise standardization.
- Do not assume every launch is about Datadog displacement.
- Do not claim a capability exists unless it is supported by current product context or the launch PRD.
